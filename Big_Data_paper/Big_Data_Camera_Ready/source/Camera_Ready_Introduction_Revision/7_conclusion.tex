\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The component studies show configuration-dependent feature benefits.
At the primary audit budget, selective auditing yields fewer downstream
underallocations but requests more memory; the direction varies across
budgets. Separately, auditing cost increases with the audit budget.
In live Bowtie2 execution, repeated inputs reduce reservations, while new
inputs reveal undercoverage despite continued learning. The extra warm
pass reduces requests and unused memory-time, but Direct-Transfer achieves
higher coverage. These observations show tradeoffs rather than a uniform
benefit from additional history. Broader conclusions require repeated
trials, additional studies, matched hardware, and complete learning and
execution cost measurements.
